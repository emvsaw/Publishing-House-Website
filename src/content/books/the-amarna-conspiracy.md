**Champagne, jazz, and a forgery worth killing for.**

Berlin, 1924. The money is new, the nights are long, and every fashionable collector wants a piece of ancient Egypt. Toni von Treuenfels is the woman they call to prove their treasures are real. She has never been very good at telling them what they want to hear.

In one week she's hired to catalogue a dead man's dazzling private museum, and a frightened student brings her proof that one of the city's grandest collections is full of brilliant fakes. Then the student vanishes. Objects begin to slip quietly out of the dead man's rooms. And the most charming people in Berlin start closing their doors.

Toni has her eye, her nerve, a small white dog who judges everyone, and a formidable Viennese housekeeper who moved in without asking. What she does not have, and does not want, is anyone who can make her laugh. Then an American pianist sits down at her table.
