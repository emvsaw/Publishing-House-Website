**She came to Spain for a fresh start. The village supplied a body.**

At fifty-two, newly divorced Goya Winters Sánchez is learning to run Casa Esperanza, her late aunt's seaside guesthouse. Her qualifications include teaching literature, spotting an unreliable narrator, and absolutely nothing involving plumbing.

Calablanca's summer festival should be her chance to settle in. Then a local commissioner collapses after the ceremonial toast—and the baker who supplied the drinks becomes a murder suspect.

Unfortunately, he's her cook Lola's cousin. And Lola considers clearing the family name very much a guesthouse responsibility.

With a historian missing and the village producing more gossip than useful answers, Goya starts asking questions—with occasional help from Daniel, a former vet who is becoming inconveniently easy to like. But the baker has been lying, her aunt left secrets of her own, and the witnesses can't agree on what happened before the toast.

Goya knows a story can sound convincing and still be false. Now she needs to find the person editing this one—before an innocent man pays for the ending.

*A sun-soaked Spanish cozy mystery with village gossip, a slow-burn romance, and a fresh start worth fighting for.*
