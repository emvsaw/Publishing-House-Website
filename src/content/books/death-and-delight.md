**She came to Spain for a fresh start. The village supplied a body, and a man she would rather not need.**

At fifty-two, newly divorced Goya Winters Sánchez is learning to run Casa Esperanza, her late aunt's seaside guesthouse. Her qualifications include teaching literature, spotting an unreliable narrator, and absolutely nothing involving plumbing.

Calablanca's summer festival should be her chance to settle in. Then a local commissioner collapses after the ceremonial toast on the harbor pier, and the baker who supplied the drinks becomes a murder suspect.

Unfortunately, he's Lola's cousin. And Lola considers clearing the family name very much a guesthouse responsibility.

Goya's reluctant ally is Daniel, a quiet former vet who knows which villagers will talk and which never will. She trusts a carefully checked account; he trusts people she wouldn't believe without footnotes. With a historian missing, the baker lying, and her aunt's papers raising uncomfortable questions, neither can solve this alone.

But Daniel is still haunted by the sale of his veterinary practice, a decision he believes cost his patients the care they deserved and his daughter her faith in him. So when a clue leads toward Celia, his silence threatens both the investigation and the fragile trust growing between them.

Goya knows a convincing story can still be false. Finding the killer means uncovering what the village is hiding. Letting Daniel closer means deciding whether she can trust him with a truth of her own.

- A fresh start at fifty-two in a Spanish seaside village

- Reluctant sleuthing partners and a fairly clued festival whodunit
- Found family around Lola's kitchen table
- Village secrets, gossip, and dry humor
