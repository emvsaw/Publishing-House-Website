**Rose-red stone, desert stars, and a dead man with two names.**

Petra, 1925. A letter addressed to her late father brings Toni von Treuenfels to the ancient city carved into the cliffs, where a charming English photographer has fallen to his death. The British say it was an accident. The case is closed.

But the dead man's journal reads like a shopping list, his books are inscribed in an alphabet he never mentioned, and the tombs he sketched are not quite as untouched as they should be. The deeper Toni digs, the more elegant the theft becomes, and the closer it runs to her own family's name.

First, though, there is Constantinople. Stranded there with the pianist she has only ever written to, Toni gets two days, one hotel, and a kiss she cannot take back.
